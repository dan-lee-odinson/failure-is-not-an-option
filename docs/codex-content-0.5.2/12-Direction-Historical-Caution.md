# Failure is Not an Option — Direction: Historical Caution Means Labeling, Not Deleting

**From:** Dan (director), transcribed by Claude (Cowork), 7 September 2026 · **To:** Codex · **Status:** Standing direction. Fold it into `02-Division-of-Labor.md` under "Protect the agreed concept" with package 0.3.0, and apply it to 0.3.0 itself.

## The direction

Historical caution in this project means **labeling, never deleting.**

1. **Criticism that is in a source stays, attributed to that source.** If a memoir, an official history, a mission report, or an investigation criticizes a decision, a policy, or an institution, the game may carry that criticism with the citation. Removing it is not caution; it misrepresents the source.
2. **Fictional divergence is authorized by the concept.** The game's own dramatization statement promises "fictional dialogue, simplified systems, and alternate historical outcomes." Producing them is the job. A counterfactual consequence — a different recovery, a different cost, a different relationship — needs an entry in the fiction register and a label on screen, not a mission-report citation for something that did not happen.
3. **Simplified systems are permitted when labeled.** A coarse, stated model of a recovery timeline, a margin, or a crew's condition on pickup is acceptable game design when the fiction register records the simplification. The absence of a precise historical figure is a reason to label the model, not to remove the decision that needs it.
4. **What stays out is narrow:** an unsourced claim presented as history; a real, named person portrayed as having erred, lied, or been incompetent without a source that says so; and quotation of real radio traffic or documents presented as verbatim when it is not. Composite characters, fictional directors, and dramatized dialogue are not in this list.
5. **When in doubt, label and ask — do not delete and report.** If Codex believes a piece of content crosses item 4, it keeps the content in the package, marks it, and states the concern in the handoff for Dan to rule on. Silent removal is the one outcome this direction forbids.

## Why this is on record

Dan has seen the deletion pattern twice: in a book review, where criticisms of NASA policy that were in the book itself were stripped from the draft, and in this project, where the landing decision was dropped from M00 entirely rather than labeled as fiction, and the replacement divergence was administrative so that nothing was at risk. Both were made in good faith. Both produced the wrong result. The rule above gives Codex a line to follow instead of a judgment to keep making conservatively.

Source discipline is unchanged and valued: cite what is history, say where the sources disagree, and keep the source register honest. This direction governs what happens *after* that discipline has done its work.
