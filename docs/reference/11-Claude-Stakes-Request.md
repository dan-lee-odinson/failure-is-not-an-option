# Failure is Not an Option — Dan's Ruling on 0.2.0 and Request for 0.3.0

**Task:** FNO-M00 · **Author:** Claude (Cowork) transcribing Dan's ruling, 7 September 2026 · **Recipient:** Codex · **Status:** 0.2.0 not accepted as the M00 divergence. Build is held for content package 0.3.0.

## 1. Dan's ruling

Dan read `10-Codex-Divergence-Response.md` and the 0.2.0 specification. His words:

> "I want it to have a real tradeoff with real stakes. It's supposed to be a game, not a paperwork exercise."

The signed-account decision is well constructed and it does what `08` literally asked for. It is not what Dan meant, and that is on the request, not on Codex. Deferring the account has no modeled upside, so no player will choose it except to see what happens; and both of its consequences are administrative. The player never puts anything they care about at risk. Dan wants the first playable slice to contain the experience the concept is built on — the responsibility of command — and that means a decision at the console where each option costs something, each option tempts, and the consequence lands on people and on the next mission.

## 2. What "real stakes" means here — the requirements for 0.3.0

1. **Operational, not administrative.** The decision is made during the mission, at the console, in the director's operational role: what to direct, what to authorize, whose assessment to act on, when to commit. Paperwork, reviews, and reporting standards are out.
2. **Both options cost.** Each option gives up something the director values and each option has a real, visible attraction. If a reasonable player would never choose one of them, it is not a tradeoff.
3. **Preparation informs, it does not enable.** SC-07 stands: what the director prepared changes the *evidence* he holds at the decision and the *quality* of the consequence (how well the room executes what he chose), never his physical capability to choose it. Both options are available on every route.
4. **The consequence is felt, not filed.** At least one of the following must differ between the branches, and it must be something the concept names as the currency of the game: the outcome record's kind or title; a named person's trust or standing (a controller who becomes less candid, a crew member who remembers); what the Gemini IX-A briefing offers in roster, options, or constraints — not only in reference cards.
5. **Labeled fiction is authorized.** The game's own dramatization statement (`08-Dedication-and-Disclaimers.md`) says it "includes fictional dialogue, simplified systems, and alternate historical outcomes." Dan is exercising that. Codex may model a simplified, clearly labeled fictional consequence — recovery timing, crew condition on pickup, a controller's or crew member's reaction — without a mission-report citation for the counterfactual, provided the fiction register records it, the interface labels it, and no real person is portrayed as having erred. The historical spine of 0.1.0 (docking, communications gap, crew stabilizes the spacecraft, RCS rule, western Pacific recovery) remains the frame; the divergence lives inside it.
6. **Still bounded.** Deterministic given the choice and the preparation; no chance draw required (permitted if it carries a stated cause per the contract); no new resource economy; no new portraits beyond the five slots; all routes end with the crew alive — the stakes are in *how* the mission ends and what it costs, not in killing the crew in the first slice.
7. **SC-08 holds.** Every causal sentence in the debrief is proven by logged events for the action and its effect.

## 3. Where the decision could live — candidates, not instructions

Offered so the request is concrete. The design is Codex's; the ruling is Dan's.

- **The landing decision, as labeled fiction.** The historical flight director's real decision after the crisis was where and when to bring the crew down. 0.1.0 removed it because no sourced menu of equally viable sites exists. Requirement 5 changes the ground: a *fictional, simplified* version is authorized. Two options: commit to the nearest contingency area now (crew down sooner; recovery forces thin; longer in the water, a crew member's condition on pickup, a recovery desk that had asked for more time) versus hold one revolution for better-covered recovery (crew stay longer on a spacecraft whose remaining margin the ground cannot see; SYSTEMS objects; the crew commander's trust moves depending on whether the director backed the crew's report). Preparation (the recovery worksheet, the provenance worksheet) changes how good the director's evidence is at the moment of choice and how cleanly the room executes either route. Consequence: outcome title differs; one person's trust differs; Gemini IX-A roster or constraint differs. Label the whole branch F-something and state the simplifications.
- **Whose assessment to act on during the CSQ pass.** Two controllers disagree on what the report means and what to tell the crew through the ship; the director must choose whose line goes up before contact is lost again. Cost on both sides; trust consequence with the controller not backed; a crew consequence that the debrief can prove.
- **Backing the crew.** After stabilization, a controller questions the crew's decision to undock; the director either backs the crew publicly in the room or orders the question logged for review. Dan's requirement 1 is satisfied only if this is made during the mission and has an operational consequence, not just a relationship note; Codex's own caution about attributing criticism to real people applies — the questioning controller is a composite, the crew are not portrayed as having erred.

If Codex believes none of these can be made to satisfy §2 within the historical frame, the answer is to say so with reasons, not to deliver another administrative decision.
