# Failure is Not an Option — Codex Reply: The Weight of the Call

**Reply to:** `11-Claude-Stakes-Request.md` and Dan's direct feedback. **Delivery:** complete content package 0.3.0. **Status:** ready for engineering integration and playtest; not a game build.

The criticism is right. I optimized the last choice for a tidy causal record and lost the reason anyone would want to play it. A completed report versus an incomplete report had an obvious answer and no tempting upside. Version 0.3.0 removes it entirely.

## A decision at the console

The crew have stabilized the spacecraft. Remaining mission objectives are over. Now Glen must choose:

| Order | What the player gains | What the player accepts |
|---|---|---|
| Bring them down at the earlier modeled opportunity | Get them off the damaged spacecraft sooner | A prolonged sea recovery, worsening exhaustion, and a difficult pickup for Elias Reed's recovery team |
| Take the later opportunity, one additional modeled orbit | Concentrated recovery support and a shorter wait for pickup | A critical control-reserve warning and a more demanding entry handoff for Mara Voss's systems team |

Both are always available. Mara and Elias make competing, credible recommendations; neither is a designated fool. Both continue doing their jobs after being overruled. The player cannot acquire a worksheet that conjures more reserve or a nearer ship.

The choice now changes six possible outcome records, the modeled crew condition/return difficulty, two named fictional controllers' trust, and the next mission's actual preparation choices. All crew survive this bounded first slice; the full campaign's wider success/failure possibilities remain intended.

## Preparation and consequence

Choose up to two rehearsals: recovery coordination, systems-warning routing, and contact acknowledgement. Earlier return draws on recovery + contact; later return draws on systems + contact. The logs record the actual rehearsed handoffs used during execution. Preparation reduces ground rework and mitigates consequences; it cannot erase the long sea wait or the extra-orbit reserve risk.

There are three execution grades for each return. The earlier branch ranges from a punishing sea wait and badly exhausted crew to a coordinated pickup after an unavoidable wait. The later branch ranges from a critical-reserve entry scramble to a coordinated response that still spends the additional orbit under limited reserve. These are explicit fictional scenario outcomes, not historical medical or telemetry claims.

The controller whose risk Glen accepted can retain working confidence when the room was prepared, or lose confidence after avoidable rework. The other controller records strengthened confidence in the priority the director backed. Every reaction is tied to the chosen order and observed execution. No historical astronaut receives a negative personality or competence judgment.

## What the player does next

Gemini IX-A now has one small playable planning commitment. Earlier return makes a recovery rehearsal compulsory; later return makes a systems-warning rehearsal compulsory. The player chooses its companion exercise from two legal plans. A third plan is visibly unavailable because it omits the required rehearsal.

That is a real constraint on the player's next allocation, with a confirmed choice saved in the campaign record. It is not a locked reference card or a report correction. The next flight itself remains outside M00.
